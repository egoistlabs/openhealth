Wearable readings are recorded on board first, so the crew and the app can use
them straight away without Earth. On the space station, the Canadian Space
Agency's Bio-Monitor works this way: after use, the astronaut plugs the
battery pack into a base, which downloads the data to Earth through the
station's communications system.

Earth always sees the hab late, never live. Data goes down in scheduled relay
passes, and NASA notes a communication delay of up to 20 minutes one way on a
Mars mission. That is why the crew must spot problems on board first.

What Ground's flight surgeon sees, by name:

- The measures each person chooses to share, with values. Nothing else from
  the check-in.
- Any measure at Act, shared or not, but without values if it is not shared.
- Wearable vitals: heart rate, heart rate variability, blood oxygen, skin
  temperature and breathing rate.
- Body map marks: place, rating, type and note.
- A summary of conversations with the companion: counts, what was logged and
  the companion's replies. Never the person's own words.
- When the companion gave the emergency notice, but not what was said.

Journals never leave the hab. The crew view inside the hab shows only
crew-level numbers, never names.
