The second step of the daily check-in is a 3D body map. To mark pain, turn the
body and tap the muscle, bone or joint that hurts. With Skin switched on, a tap
marks the skin instead: a rash, bite or sting, cut or scrape, bruise, burn,
itch, dry or cracked skin, blister or swelling. If nothing hurts, tap No pain
today.

Each mark needs three things before the check-in moves on:

1. A rating from 1 to 10. 1 to 3 is mild, 4 to 6 moderate, 7 to 10 severe.
2. A short note on when it happens, for example during exercise or after
   waking. It can be spoken.
3. For a skin mark, what it is.

Marks from earlier sols glow on the body, so a pain that keeps coming back is
easy to see. From Home, a mark can be flagged for follow-up. The next check-in
then asks whether it is still there.

The body map records where and how much. It does not find the cause. NASA notes
that crews exercise to counteract losses in bone density and muscle strength,
so a sore muscle after exercise is common. Tell the crew medical officer about
any pain that is severe, new and unexplained, getting worse, or still there
after a few sols.

Ground's flight surgeon can see each mark: the place, the rating, the type and
the note.
