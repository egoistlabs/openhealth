The body map is on Pedro's screen, not in the daily check-in. Open it with the
Body map button at the top of Pedro's screen, or Open body map in the Body map
tab of the log. Telling Pedro that something hurts, aches or is injured, or
that there is a skin problem, opens it too. Pedro moves to the side and talks
you through it.

To mark pain, drag to turn the body and tap the muscle, bone or joint that
hurts. The area buttons along the bottom jump to a part, like Shoulder or Knee.
With Skin switched on, a tap marks the skin instead: a rash, bite or sting, cut
or scrape, bruise, burn, itch, dry or cracked skin, blister or swelling. If
nothing hurts, tap No pain today.

Each mark needs three things before it can be saved:

1. A rating from 1 to 10. 1 to 3 is mild, 4 to 6 moderate, 7 to 10 severe.
2. A short note on when it happens, for example during exercise or after
   waking. Type it in the panel, or hold the mic and tell Pedro, for example
   "sharp when I lift my arm, about six out of ten". Saying save saves it.
3. For a skin mark, what it is.

Press Save to my log. The mark appears in the conversation log with the body
picture and your note, and in the Body map tab. Marks from earlier sols glow on
the body, so a pain that keeps coming back is easy to see.

To track how it heals, press Follow up next time on the mark in the log, or
flag it from the Body card on Home. The next time you open the body map, Pedro
asks whether it is still there and how it has changed.

The body map records where and how much. It does not find the cause. NASA notes
that crews exercise to counteract losses in bone density and muscle strength,
so a sore muscle after exercise is common. Tell the crew medical officer about
any pain that is severe, new and unexplained, getting worse, or still there
after a few sols.

Ground's flight surgeon can see each mark: the place, the rating, the type and
the note.
