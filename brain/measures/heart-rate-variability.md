Heart rate variability (HRV) is how much the time between heartbeats changes.
The crew wearable reads it overnight, in milliseconds.

HRV is very personal. An HRV that is normal in one person may not be normal
for someone else, and comparing a person's current HRV with their past values
is what makes it useful. So the app compares each night only with that
person's own normal from the previous 14 nights, never with other crew and
never with a fixed number. Application code sets Stable, Watch or Urgent. The
companion must not calculate the status itself.

Readings from wrist-worn trackers are less precise than a medical recording,
so one night means little. A run of nights below someone's own normal often
goes with short sleep, stress or a heavy workload.

On Watch: suggest protecting sleep and easing the workload where the mission
plan allows. On Urgent, or if the person also feels unwell: tell the crew
medical officer. A low HRV on its own is not a diagnosis.
