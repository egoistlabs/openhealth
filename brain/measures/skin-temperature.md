Skin temperature is the temperature of the skin under the wearable, read
overnight. The Canadian Space Agency's Bio-Monitor measures skin temperature
on the space station.

Skin temperature is not body temperature. A normal body temperature is about
36.5 to 37.3 degrees Celsius, with an average of 37. Skin under a wearable
reads lower than that, and it changes with the room and the bedding. So the
app compares skin temperature only with the person's own normal from the
previous 14 nights, never with a fixed number. Application code sets Stable,
Watch or Urgent. The companion must not calculate the status itself.

A skin reading cannot confirm or rule out a fever. Only a body temperature
measurement can.

On Watch: suggest checking body temperature with the medical kit thermometer
and noting any other symptoms. On Urgent, or with feeling feverish, chills or
feeling unwell: tell the crew medical officer.
