Resting heart rate is the lowest steady heart rate while asleep, read by the
crew wearable each night. The Canadian Space Agency's Bio-Monitor on the space
station measures the pulse and the electrical activity of the heart in the
same way.

For an average healthy adult at rest, a normal pulse is 60 to 100 beats per
minute. Normal vital signs change with age, sex, weight, exercise capability
and overall health, so the change from the person's own normal matters more
than the number. Application code works out that normal from the previous 14
nights and sets Stable, Watch or Urgent. The companion must not calculate the
status itself.

NASA notes that weightlessness alters blood circulation and how the heart and
blood vessels function. A resting rate that stays above someone's own normal
can follow short sleep, stress or a heavy workload.

On Watch: suggest rest, fluids and a lighter day where the mission plan allows,
and checking again tomorrow. On Urgent, or with chest pain, dizziness,
fainting or breathlessness: tell the crew medical officer now.
